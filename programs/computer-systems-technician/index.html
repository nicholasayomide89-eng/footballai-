Computer Systems Technician

A one-year, three-semester certificate that prepares you for an entry-level career in IT: repairing computers, supporting users, running networks and keeping systems secure. It ends with a six-week field placement and prepares you to write the CompTIA A+ certification exams.

01

About the program

Graduates can work on their own or as part of a team to set up and maintain IT solutions for the day-to-day needs of individuals and organizations. The work ranges from repairing desktop computers to monitoring network operations, in large and small organizations across many sectors.

The program covers the technical side of common components and troubleshooting techniques for computer hardware, operating systems, networking, system administration and related support and application software. It also builds communication and administration skills, including teamwork, project management and customer service.

Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit

The program integrates the Guiding Principles of Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit and values the collaborative learning environments unique to Nunavut. Instructors balance learning labs and project-based assignments with conventional lectures.

Inuuqatigiitsiarniq

Respecting others' work and developing healthy work relationships with fellow workers.

Pilimmaksarniq

Developing skills through practice, effort, action and patience.

Tunnganarniq

Inclusive participation through open dialogue and continuous feedback, solving problems together.

Career opportunities

Graduates may work as technical support specialists for computers, mobile devices and networks in industrial, government and service sectors.

  • Technical support specialist
  • System administration
  • Network administration (LAN/WAN)
  • Internet / intranet maintenance
  • Hardware & software sales
  • Customer service support
  • Computer repair

How it's delivered

Blended delivery: applied learning, laboratory work, practicums, classroom teaching, seminars and workshops, guest speakers and Elders, and field trips and project work that connect you with the community. Some digital learning is used in courses throughout the program.

02

What you'll be able to do

When you finish the program, you will be able to:

  1. Identify, analyze, develop, implement, verify and document the requirements for a computing environment.
  2. Diagnose, troubleshoot, document and monitor technical problems with appropriate methods and tools.
  3. Implement and maintain secure computing environments.
  4. Communicate and collaborate with team members and stakeholders to build effective working relationships.
  5. Select and apply strategies for personal and professional development.
  6. Apply project management principles and tools to projects in a computing environment.
  7. Follow ethical, legal and regulatory requirements when developing and managing computing solutions.
  8. Provide end-to-end customer support, from identifying problems to troubleshooting and debugging.
  9. Design, install, configure, maintain, upgrade and decommission computing infrastructure.
  10. Perform day-to-day IT support: computer assembly, wireless networking, installing programs, mobile device troubleshooting.
  11. Provide technical support for computing infrastructure in line with industry best practice.
  12. Compare cloud computing solutions: SaaS, PaaS and IaaS.
03

Program structure

One year over three semesters, including a six-week field placement. There are no electives. Select a course to read its description.

Sem 1 · Sep–DecSem 2 · Jan–AprSem 3 · 6 wks

Semester 1 · September – December

270 h · 18 credits
CST 101Technical Communications45 h · 3 cr

Clear, concise and detailed communication is essential in technical workplaces. You will plan and carry out a range of formal and informal visual, oral and written communication tasks, with exercises designed to build confidence and competence in workplace communication.

CST 102Math for IT: Numeracy and Logic45 h · 3 cr

A review of mathematical fundamentals, percentages and markups, cash flow calculations, one-time and recurring costs and benefits, present value of cash flows, return on investment (ROI) and net present value (NPV).

CST 103Computer Fundamentals60 h · 4 cr

The first of a series preparing you for a role as an entry-level IT support specialist. It introduces the facets of IT (computer hardware, the Internet, software, troubleshooting and customer service) and gives an overview of the rest of the program.

CST 104Introduction to Networking60 h · 4 cr

A full overview of computer networking, from the fundamentals of modern networking technologies and protocols to the cloud, practical applications and network troubleshooting.

CST 105Operating Systems60 h · 4 cr

Through video lectures, demonstrations and hands-on practice, you will learn the main components of an operating system and how to perform critical tasks such as managing software and users and configuring hardware.

Semester 2 · January – April

285 h · 19 credits
CST 106System Administration and IT Infrastructure Services60 h · 4 cr

Moves you from working on a single computer to managing an entire fleet in a multi-user environment. It covers the infrastructure services that keep organizations running, a deep dive on cloud computing and managing cloud resources, server configuration, and industry tools for managing computers, user information and productivity.

CST 107IT Security Fundamentals60 h · 4 cr

IT security concepts, tools and best practices: threats and attacks, encryption algorithms, the three As (authentication, authorization and accounting), and network security from firewalls to Wi-Fi encryption. It ends by combining these into a multi-layered security architecture and building a culture of security in a team.

CST 108Foundations of IT Service Management45 h · 3 cr

IT Service Management as described in the IT Infrastructure Library (ITIL): common structures and best practices for working on a service team, plus soft skills such as effective listening and communication with customers who have IT issues and requests.

CST 109PC Troubleshooting60 h · 4 cr

Protecting the integrity of data: safe practices online, malicious threats such as phishing and hacking, managing software and hardware patches and updates, and firewall and anti-malware software, so you can protect personal and corporate data and block unauthorized access.

CST 110A+ Certification Preparation60 h · 4 cr

The skills required for CompTIA A+, the industry standard certification for IT professionals. Lab activities cover installing, maintaining and troubleshooting PC and mobile hardware, networking and connectivity, and supporting a variety of operating systems. At the end you will be ready to write the qualifying exams.

Semester 3 · April – June (6-week block)

195 h · 6 credits
CST 111Field Placement195 h · 6 cr

You take part in the full range of tasks of an IT support position while receiving mentoring and coaching. It is a culminating, performance-based assessment focused on applying your knowledge, critical thinking, problem-solving, teamwork, oral communication and literacy. At the end you submit a written report documenting your tasks and reflecting on your growth and learning goals.

04

Field placement

The work-integrated Field Placement is a key part of the program. It lets you observe and build the practical and professional skills needed for a career as an IT professional, and apply what you learned in class in real IT workplaces.

  • Complete detailed learning contracts and a reflective self-evaluation.
  • Achieve an acceptable overall placement evaluation.
  • Attend a preparation seminar and meet industry representatives before you start.
  • Deliver a presentation at the end of the placement.
Credits and eligibility1 credit is given for every 30 hours of field time, for a total of 6 credits over the six-week block. You must complete all courses in the earlier semesters to qualify for placement.
05

Admission & graduation

Applicants must

  • Have a Nunavut High School diploma (general or advanced), ABE Math 140 and ABE English 140, or equivalent. Math and English assessments may be required.
  • Be 17 years of age or older.
  • Submit a letter explaining their interest in the program and reasons for applying.
  • Provide 2 references (not family members).

Applying through prior learning assessment

  • Submit a recent resume detailing your work history.
  • Include a letter of reference from an employer or community organization.
  • Eligibility is determined by academic achievement testing in English and Math.

Selection process

  • Application documents are reviewed.
  • Math and English assessment results are reviewed.
  • You may be contacted for an interview in person or by telephone.
  • Admission depends on space and meeting entrance requirements; some students may need to meet special conditions first. Preference is given to Nunavut Inuit.

To graduate

  • Earn at least 50% in every course (Field Placement is pass/fail).
  • Maintain a weighted average of 60% to be promoted through each semester and to graduate.
